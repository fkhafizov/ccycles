A shortest path in Gp cuts R(X,Y) into pieces

Walk from X to Y along a shortest path. Each move changes the column heights ai and adds or removes a piece of the region between the two staircases. In Gp one letter jumps over p − 1 others, so a piece is a vertical bar of p − 1 cells, a horizontal bar of p − 1 cells, a single square, or a mixed piece; for p = 3 the bars are vertical and horizontal dominoes.

How this works

The graph Gp(k,n). Vertices are binary words of length n with k zeros. Two words are adjacent when one is obtained from the other by a cyclic shift of p consecutive letters, e.g. for p = 3: 011 ↦ 110, 001 ↦ 100, 010 ↦ 100. In such a move one letter jumps over its p − 1 neighbours.

Columns. If zi is the position of the i-th zero (counted from 1), then ai = zi − i is the number of 1s to its left. Column i of the picture has height ai. The staircase is the lattice path of the word: 1 = up, 0 = right.

The region. R(X,Y) is the set of squares (i,r) with min(ai(X), ai(Y)) ≤ r < max(ai(X), ai(Y)), shaded light blue. Hence |R| = Σ|ai(X) − ai(Y)|.

Pieces. Every move changes some ai; the squares between the old and new heights form a piece. For p = 3:

  • a 0 jumps over two 1s (011 ↔ 110): one column changes by 2, a vertical domino;
  • a 1 jumps over two 0s (001 ↔ 100): two neighbouring columns change by 1, a horizontal domino;
  • a letter moves by one (e.g. 010 ↦ 100): one column changes by 1, a single square.

The number in a piece is the step at which it appears. Hatched pieces are removed (the column gets shorter); plain pieces are added. If every square of R is touched exactly once, d(X,Y) = number of pieces. The Numbers panel flags squares touched twice or outside R.

Controls. Build path computes BFS distances in Gp(k,n) and picks a shortest path from X to Y. Start animates it, Step and Back move by one, Reset returns to X, Another path draws a new random shortest path. Keyboard: space starts or pauses, ← and → step.

Numbers. ν(R) is the maximum number of disjoint dominoes inside R. For p = 3 the panel compares d(X,Y) with |R| − ν(R).
